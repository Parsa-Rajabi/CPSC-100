## Part A: Your Digital Footprint

Individual. Done in Lab 3. Due Thursday Oct 8 at 11:59pm.

We give you a pre-built Exposure Index calculator in Snap!. It asks 7 things about your online life and returns 1 number. Partway through lab the class votes on which factors should count for more, your TA re-weights the calculator, and you run it again with the same answers.

What you do:

1. Run the calculator and record your Round 1 score.
2. Annotate 2 parts of the script.
3. Vote on the weights.
4. Run it again and record your Round 2 score.
5. Compare with a partner.

Rules:

- You may only change the tier weight variables at the top of the script, the ones the class votes on. Everything else stays identical or the 2 rounds mean nothing.
- Share the number, never the answers. "My photo content factor was a big driver" is a complete answer.

Deliverable: a short individual reflection, a few paragraphs, answering:

1. What factors affected your score the most?
2. How did your score differ from your partner's?
3. What does the gap between Round 1 and Round 2 reveal about the Index?
4. What is one thing the calculator should have factored in, and why?

Question 4 carries the most weight.

## Part B: The AI Detector Experiment

Group. Runs Weeks 2 to 6. Due Thursday Oct 15 at 11:59pm.

You test how well AI-detection tools actually work. Every group works with images.

### Build a dataset

Selection due Thursday Oct 1. Your TA signs it off in Lab 3, Week 5.

- We provide 4 starter images: 2 real, 2 AI-generated.
- Each member adds 2 more: 1 real, 1 AI-generated.
- Document where every item came from and how you know which is which.
- Include at least 1 camera photo lightly edited with a non-AI editor, 1 building, and 1 nature scene.
- All images legible, at least 72 ppi.

### Choose your tools

- Shortlist about 10, then narrow: 1 tool per group member, 3 minimum.
- Write down why you picked those.
- Spread your picks. Most review sites name the same 1 or 2 tools, so groups left alone converge on the same handful.

Starter list, free and browser-accessible as of mid-2026. Recheck free tiers before committing.

| Approach | Tools |
| :------- | :---- |
| Pattern-based | Hive Moderation, Illuminarty, AI or Not, WasItAI, DeepAI, ZeroGPT, NoteGPT, Winston AI, Content at Scale |
| Provenance metadata | Optic, which checks C2PA data instead of scanning for patterns |

### Run the experiment

- Set up tool access before Lab 4: accounts made, limits checked, logins tested.
- Start running your dataset in Lab 4. TAs circulate throughout.
- You will not finish in lab, and you are not expected to.

### Read the terms of service

For each tool, answer 3 questions:

1. Does it keep what you upload, and for how long?
2. Can it use your submissions to train future models?
3. Does it share submissions with third parties?

> [!TIP]
> Search each policy for: retain, store, delete, train, improve our services, third part, share, disclose. "Improve our services" is often doing the work of "train our models". Check whether the privacy policy and the terms of service disagree.

### Go deeper on 1 tool

How does it claim to work, how did it do on your dataset, and where specifically did it fail?

### Deliverable

- Your dataset, with collection notes.
- A results table across all tools.
- Your tool selection justification.
- The 3 terms-of-service answers, per tool.
- A recommendation: which of your tools would you recommend to your family, and why?
- A reflection: what does a false positive cost, what does a false negative cost, and who pays in each case?
- An appendix of result screenshots with your computer clock visible.

## Part C: Algorithmic Bias Case Study

Group. Runs Weeks 6 to 7. Due Thursday Oct 22 at 11:59pm.

A written report on a real, documented case where an algorithmic system caused harm.

- Pick a case connected to Canada or a country in Asia.
- Pick it after the Algorithmic Bias lecture on Oct 14.
- **No two groups in the same lab section may take the same case.** Cases are first come, first served: claim yours by replying to the Part C thread on Ed Discussion, and check the replies before you settle on one.
- Documented means a regulator's report, court filings, or investigative journalism. Not a blog post asserting something happened.
- Name the mechanism using course vocabulary: decision tree, weighted scoring, feedback loop, training-data gap, proxy variable.

Deliverable: the template below, 800 to 1200 words.

### Part C report template

Fill in every section. Bullet points are fine. Header: case name, country, group members.

1. What happened. 1 paragraph: what the system was, who deployed it, what went wrong. Written so somebody unfamiliar with the case can follow.
2. Input. What data went in, where it came from, and whether the people it described agreed to this use.
3. Decision logic. What the system did with that data to reach an outcome. Not source code. "A submitted photo is matched against a scraped database to identify a person" is the right level.
4. Outcome. What happened, to whom, and any response: a regulator's finding, a ruling, a company change.
5. Stakeholders and effects. A table of at least 3 stakeholders, each with 1 positive and 1 negative effect.
6. Benjamin's categories. Which of the 4 fits: engineered inequity, default discrimination, coded exposure, technological benevolence. Say why, pointing at facts from sections 2 to 4. Then give a plausible alternative reading: which other category someone could argue for, and on what grounds. This is required.
7. The mechanism. Name the course concept and explain the connection in 2 or 3 sentences.
8. Should they change it? 1 argument for and 1 against, both grounded in a real trade-off.
9. Sources. At least 3, at least 2 primary. News coverage counts as 1 source, not 3.
