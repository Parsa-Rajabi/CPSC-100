## Overview

Part C is where your group shows, in person, that it understands the app it built, including the
code Copilot wrote.

A viva voce is an oral exam. Your group demonstrates its app, explains its design decisions, and
answers our questions aloud.

The goal is that every member can explain any part of your app: what it does, why you designed it
that way, and how you tested it.

- Part C is group work, worth 10% of your course grade
- Your group presents in person, in one session in Week 13. The dates are in
  [What you hand in](project-2.md#what-you-hand-in) on the Project 2 page
- Attending is mandatory, and it is a [passing requirement](syllabus.md#passing-criteria)

### Before you start <!-- {docsify-ignore} -->

Before reading the instructions for this part of the project, review the [rubric](#grading-criteria),
especially the A+ / Exceeds Expectations category.

As a group, discuss which grade bracket you are aiming for and what it will take to get there. Be
realistic about everyone's availability and willingness to put in the work. Do not overpromise and
underdeliver to each other. Agree on expectations that everyone understands and can commit to.

To reach the A+ / Exceeds Expectations category, your submission must meet all of the
expectations and demonstrate the additional depth and attention to detail described in the rubric.
Plan for these expectations from the beginning. They are difficult to address well as last-minute
additions.

These criteria have been chosen deliberately, and TAs will look for clear evidence of them in your
work.

### What happens <!-- {docsify-ignore} -->

- Demo. Show your app running, and show each of its main features
- Explain your choices. Justify your design and code decisions
- Questions. The teaching team asks questions. Any member may be asked about any part of the app,
  not only the part they built

We will announce these details closer to the date:

- how long each session is
- how to book your session
- where it takes place
- what you may bring

## Details

Before your session, re-read your design requirements document and your testing report.

### Demonstration <!-- {docsify-ignore} -->

To prepare your demo:

- Test your demo on the device you will present with
- Decide who demonstrates which feature
- Practise moving from one speaker to the next

### Design decisions and course concepts <!-- {docsify-ignore} -->

We ask about your design decisions:

- Who is your user, and how does each feature help them?
- Why did you design it this way instead of another way?
- How does your app use the course concepts you chose?

Connect your answers to your user. "Copilot suggested it" is not enough as a reason. "We kept it
because it makes the next meal easier for our user to find" is a reason.

### Testing and bugs <!-- {docsify-ignore} -->

We ask about testing and finding bugs:

- How did you decide what to test?
- What methods did you use to find bugs, such as unexpected input, clicking every button, or
  having someone else try the app?
- Tell us about a bug you found. How did you find it, and how did you fix it?

### Changes along the way <!-- {docsify-ignore} -->

We ask about the changes you made while building:

- What changed from your design requirements document, and why?
- Where did Copilot make a mistake, and how did you notice? If it never did, how did you check
  its code?

### Delivery <!-- {docsify-ignore} -->

- Make sure every member can explain every part of the app, including code they did not prompt for
- Speak, do not read. A few words of notes are fine. Do not read a full answer aloud from a script,
  your documents or your slides
- Speak so that everyone in the room can hear you. If you do not hear or understand a question, ask
  us to repeat it
- Use your documents as evidence. When you mention a feature or a test, name it by its number, such
  as F2 or T3
- Practise together before your session, so that your demo runs without restarts or pauses longer
  than 10 seconds, and each member continues from where the last one stopped

## Regrading criteria

Because the viva voce is a live presentation, regrading is inherently difficult and may not be
possible without supporting evidence. If your group believes there is a chance you may wish to
pursue a regrading request for the viva voce, you are responsible for recording your presentation
using one of your group members' laptops (for example, by recording via Zoom).

Having a recording provides the teaching team with a concrete reference to review the presentation
again and determine whether the grading criteria were applied correctly. Without a recording, the
teaching team may be unable to reassess the presentation, and a regrading request may not be
considered. Any regrading requests must still follow the course regrading policy and timelines
outlined in the [remarking policy](syllabus.md#remarking-policy).

The teaching team agrees on how to mark before the sessions, so every group is marked to the same
standard.
