## What Labs Are For

Labs are not a parallel stream of unrelated worksheets. Each one builds a piece of what your [project](project.md) needs, and gives you TA time to check that piece **before** it becomes a graded milestone.

That means a lab is the cheapest place in this course to find out you have misunderstood something. Use it that way.

Labs are also where the programming happens. The course moves from **Snap!** early in the term to **Python** later, and labs are where you actually write things rather than watch someone else write them.

## How Labs Are Graded

Labs are graded on **completion**, not on getting everything right the first time. The point is that you did the work and engaged with it.

Two separate things are recorded each week:

| What | Counts toward |
| :--- | :------------ |
| Completing the lab work | **Labs**, 7% (this page) |
| Being present and taking part | [**Course Participation**](participation.md), 3% |

So skipping a lab costs you twice. Both components have built-in flexibility, but they are tracked independently.

## Coming to Lab

- **Arrive on time.** Much of the lab work is group-based, and a late arrival holds up other people.
- **Do any listed pre-work.** Labs assume you arrive ready; check the [Schedule](schedule.md).
- **Bring your laptop.**
- **Come with questions.** The TAs are there for exactly that.

## If You Miss a Lab

Ordinary short-term absences are covered by the built-in flexibility in the lab and participation components. You do not need to email us about a normal missed lab.

If an ongoing or serious circumstance affects several parts of the course, follow the [academic concession](syllabus.md#academic-concessions) process and let the teaching team know. Do not wait until the end of term.
