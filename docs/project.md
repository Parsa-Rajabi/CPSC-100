## Overview

The course project runs the whole term and is where most of what you learn gets used on something real. There are two projects, equally weighted, and both are group work.

Groups are 4 to 5 students, assigned by the instructor rather than chosen. They come out of the **group formation activity in Lab 1** and the **group formation survey** due Friday Sep 18. **Groups do not span lab sections** — everyone in your group attends the same section, so you share lab time and have the same lead TA to go to.

| | Project | Weeks | Ends with |
| :-: | :------ | :---: | :-------- |
| 1 | [Signal or Noise?](project-1.md) | 2 to 7 | 3 submitted parts |
| 2 | [From Blocks to Code](project-2.md) | 8 to 13 | A viva voce on Dec 2 |

> [!WARNING]
> To pass this course you must achieve at least **50% on the course project**. See [Course Evaluation](syllabus.md#course-evaluation) for how much the project is worth.

## How the project is graded

Four things make up your project grade. The **project parts** are the documents you hand in, and they carry the most weight. **Weekly stand-ups** are three or four sentences posted each week saying what you did, what is next, and what is blocking you. **Retrospectives** are short group write-ups after each project about what worked and what you would change. And **peer evaluation** acts as a multiplier on Part C of each project, which is what lets people in the same group end up with different grades.

How the grade divides across those four will be announced soon.

### The 5 bands

Everything you submit lands in one of 5 bands rather than being marked out of a percentage.

| Band | Mark | What it means |
| :--- | :--: | :------------ |
| Incomplete | 0 to 40 | Not there, or too fragmentary to assess |
| Developing | 65 | Present, but does not answer what was asked |
| Meets requirements | 80 | Does what was asked, accurately |
| Exceeds requirements | 90 | Meets, plus you noticed something nobody asked for |
| Exceptional | 100 | Exceeds, plus you pursued it |

Doing everything correctly earns 80, which at UBC is an A-. The top two bands are not for polish; they are for noticing something, and Exceeds requires Meets first, so a good tangent does not make up for a missing section.

| Situation | Result |
| :-------- | :----- |

### Where to submit

Project parts and retrospectives go to **Canvas**. Weekly stand-ups are posted on **Ed Discussion**. Peer evaluations run in **iPeer**, UBC's tool for rating teammates' contribution. Lab work is submitted separately through PrairieLearn and occasionally Canvas — see the [Labs](labs.md) page.

The tools themselves are the ones you already use in lab: **Snap!** at [snap.berkeley.edu](https://snap.berkeley.edu), which runs in your browser, and **Jupyter** for Python later in the term. Scheduling for the viva voce will be announced closer to the date.

### Late work

> [!ATTENTION]
> Late work is not accepted on the project. Nothing submitted after a deadline is marked.

Submit a partial document rather than nothing — an incomplete submission earns marks, and a missing one does not. If something serious or ongoing is happening, use the [academic concession](syllabus.md#academic-concessions) process and tell us early rather than afterwards.

## Group process

### Group contract

Your contract sets out roles, expectations, how you communicate, and how you resolve conflict. Name specific behaviours, timelines, and consequences: "We will communicate well" is not a contract.

> [!ATTENTION]
> Every contract is rejected on the first round, by design. It is how the exercise works, not a comment on your group.

You get one free revision after that, and each further resubmission costs 1% of your course grade. The template and guidance are on [Group Work Resources](group-work-resources.md).

### Weekly stand-ups

Each student posts their own stand-up on **Ed Discussion** every week from Week 2 to Week 13, by **Friday 4:00pm**. Three or four sentences is the whole thing: what you did, what is next, what is blocking you. They are graded as part of your project mark.

Posting on Ed rather than sending them to us means your group and your TA can both see them, so it is the cheapest way to flag a blocker before it becomes a problem.

### Retrospectives and peer evaluation

A retrospective is a short group write-up after finishing something: what worked, what did not, what you would change. Your group submits three, each paired with a round of peer evaluation.

| Round | Week | Retrospective covers | Peer evaluation |
| :---- | :--: | :------------------- | :-------------- |
| Project 0 | 4 | Forming your group and writing the contract | iPeer 0, practice, not graded |
| Project 1 | 8 | [Signal or Noise?](project-1.md) | iPeer 1, graded |
| Project 2 | 13 | [From Blocks to Code](project-2.md) | iPeer 2, graded |

Peer evaluations run in iPeer and act as a multiplier on **Part C** of each project. Your teammates' ratings do not move your mark on the other parts. See [Project Peer Evaluation](project-peer-evaluation.md) for the formula and criteria.

### If contribution is uneven

By default everyone in a group receives the same grade. If lab records and peer evaluations agree that somebody contributed significantly less, that person's grade may be adjusted down.

The important thing is to raise problems early, because fixing a group takes one to two weeks to actually work. Leaving it until the week before a deadline means there is no time left for anything to change.

<details>
<summary><strong>How to escalate a problem in your group</strong></summary>

1. Talk to your teammates, using the process in your contract. Keep a record of what you tried.
2. Bring it to your TA with that record if talking did not fix it.
3. Ask us to step in if the TA cannot resolve it. Contact us **at least 12 days before the deadline**, and set up a meeting **at least 9 days before it** with everyone present.

Full details are in the [syllabus](syllabus.md#importance-of-group-work-and-participation-policy) and on [Group Work Resources](group-work-resources.md#conflict-resolution).

</details>

## Academic integrity and AI

Fabrication means presenting something as true when it is not: citing a source that does not exist, reporting results you did not obtain, describing events that did not happen, or misrepresenting what a real source says. A wrong page number or a broken link is an error, not fabrication.

> [!WARNING]
> Fabrication means **0 on the project** for the student responsible, and 0 on the affected part for the rest of the group. If it is not possible to establish who fabricated it, the **0 on the project applies to every member of the group**, since the work was submitted jointly.

The way to stay clear of this is unglamorous: open every source before you submit and confirm it exists and says what you say it says. [Lab 5](labs.md) includes a verification step for exactly this.

AI use is governed by the [AI Policy](ai-policy.md), and an AI usage disclosure is required in both project submissions. Whatever tools you use, you remain responsible for the accuracy of what you hand in, and you should be able to explain any work submitted under your name.
