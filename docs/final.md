## What the Final Exam Is For

There is no midterm in this course. The eight quizzes are your recurring individual checkpoints through the term, and the final exam is the **cumulative synthesis** assessment.

That means the final is not a bigger version of a quiz. It asks you to:

- connect ideas across the three parts of the course;
- apply concepts to situations you have not seen before;
- interpret, trace, and debug;
- explain your reasoning, not just recall a definition.

## How to Prepare Well

The final exam assesses your understanding of course ideas and your ability to reason with them. A good preparation plan includes both studying and taking care of yourself.

| What to do | Why it helps |
| :--------- | :----------- |
| Review the full course arc, all three parts | The final draws connections across topics from the whole term. |
| Revisit your project work | Many ideas become clearer when connected to decisions you actually made and defended. |
| Redo quiz questions without looking at answers | The final rewards reasoning you can reproduce, not answers you recognize. |
| Practice explaining concepts in your own words | Many questions require explanation, not recognition of terms. |
| Work through the [practice problems](practice/index.html) | Extra reps on the kinds of reasoning the exam asks for. |
| Sleep the night before | Rest improves attention, memory, and judgment during the exam. |
| Eat, hydrate, and plan your route | Do not make the exam harder with preventable stressors. |
| Pack your UBC ID, pencil, and eraser the night before | These are required, and packing early reduces day-of friction. |

Useful UBC support links:

- [UBC stress resources](https://students.ubc.ca/health/health-topics/understanding-stress/)

## Before You Enter the Room

Do not enter the exam room until you are told to do so.

When you arrive:

1. Wait outside the room until instructed to enter.
2. Place all belongings, including your bag, at the front or sides of the room.
3. Turn off all electronic devices.
4. Keep only permitted exam materials with you.

Electronic devices include, but are not limited to:

- phones
- headphones or earbuds
- electronic watches
- electronic glasses
- any device that can record, transmit, store, display, or receive information

<details>
<summary><strong>Electronic glasses and wearable technology</strong></summary>

Because camera-enabled and AR glasses are increasingly common, any glasses or wearable items suspected of containing technology such as cameras, microphones, buttons, displays, or recording/transmission capability may be reviewed by the teaching team.

If a non-permitted device is found, the student must put it away with their belongings. Students who refuse to put away a non-permitted device will not be allowed to write the exam.

</details>

## Centre for Accessibility Arrangements

Students registered with the Centre for Accessibility are responsible for arranging their exam accommodations through the Centre for Accessibility office.

You must also confirm your exam arrangement by emailing the **course admin address**, listed on the [Course Communication](communication.md) page.

Contact the teaching team **10 to 7 days before the final exam** once the final exam date is announced.

If you do not make and confirm arrangements, the teaching team will assume that you are writing the final exam with the rest of the class.

## Exam Viewing and Regrading

Students may request an in-person viewing of their final exam. The exact date and time will be announced.

During an exam viewing, students may review their exam with a member of the teaching team. Students may not take photos, make copies, or otherwise reproduce any part of the exam.

<details>
<summary><strong>Regrading requests</strong></summary>

Regrade requests will only be available during the announced request window. After that window closes, and in line with the course grading policy, grades will be considered final and further grade changes or grade discussions will not be available.

Refer to the [syllabus remarking policy](syllabus.md#remarking-policy) for the course-level process.

</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Review of Assigned Standing (RAS)</strong></summary>

If you are not satisfied with your assigned standing, you are encouraged to first discuss your standing with your course instructor, when possible.

If you still believe that your assigned standing has not been correctly evaluated, you may apply for a [Review of Assigned Standing (RAS)](https://vancouver.calendar.ubc.ca/campus-wide-policies-and-regulations/review-assigned-standing) through the university student services.

More details are available on the [UBC grades page](https://students.ubc.ca/enrolment/courses/grades/).

</details>

## If You Cannot Attend

If an emergency or serious unexpected situation prevents you from taking the final exam, contact your faculty advising office as soon as possible and follow the university's academic concession process. You should also inform the teaching team, but students are expected to follow the university process for concessions.

You must contact [UBC Science](https://science.ubc.ca/students/advising) within **48 hours** of a missed final exam to apply for a deferred exam.

Email the **course staff address**, listed on the [Course Communication](communication.md) page, if you cannot attend, may be running late, or are dealing with an emergency that affects the exam. Include your full name, student number, what is happening, and whether you have already contacted your faculty advising office.

The sooner you reach out, the more options may be available, within reason and depending on the situation.

## Help and Mental Health Support

| Resource | When to use it |

| [UBC Counselling Services](https://students.ubc.ca/health/counselling-services/) | Mental health support, stress, anxiety, test anxiety, or help deciding what support you need. |
| [Here2Talk](https://here2talk.ca/) | Free 24/7 mental health counselling by phone, app, or online chat for BC post-secondary students. |
| [UBC Student Assistance Program](https://students.ubc.ca/health/ubc-student-assistance-program-sap) | Free 24/7 wellness support for UBC students. |
| [UBC Student Health Service](https://students.ubc.ca/health/student-health-service) | Medical or health concerns. |
